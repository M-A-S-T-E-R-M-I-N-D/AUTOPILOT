## 1. Abstract

AUTOPILOT is an autonomous engineering agent that flies its own repository: each *firing* orients, picks one unit of
work from a prioritized board, implements it, runs the project's gate (typecheck + lint + test + build), and commits
— or reverts and reports a no-op. Every firing is recorded to a local SQLite store with a mix of self-reported fields
(cost, token counts, the agent's own outcome tag) and mechanically-verified fields (whether the gate passed, whether
the named commit SHA actually landed on HEAD). This document is the running self-study of that process: what the
telemetry says happened, read against the threats to validity that come from a system studying itself.

### Findings at a glance

The five conclusions this study currently supports, each traceable to a mechanically-generated table in §4, the
discussion in §5, or a cited in-repo evaluation — all read under §6's single-subject caveats:

1. **Ship rate scaled WITH volume, not against it.** Across the archived 1,142-firing arc, the 3-day rolling ship
   rate climbed 65.6% → 96.9% while daily firing volume multiplied (32 → 89 firings/day at peak) — reliability rose
   as throughput rose (§5 RQ1). Likely mechanism: the gate-then-commit-or-revert loop, where a failed firing reverts
   instead of landing.
2. **Human guidance concentrates at ADMISSION, not execution.** Once a task is on the board, operator-authored and
   agent-proposed tasks shipped at near-identical rates and costs (94.2% vs 92.3%; $3.61 vs $2.13/solved, thin
   26-firing sample) — while every recorded operator verdict acts on what ENTERS the board (§5 RQ2).
3. **Newer prompts are not cheaper prompts.** Cost per shipped firing and median turns ROSE after firing-v8.1
   ($2.80 → $4.43/solved; 45 → 66.5 median turns) even as pass rate held ≥95% — optimizing pass rate alone selects
   for expensive, high-variance configurations (§5 RQ3, SOTA-MAP H3).
4. **The one mechanically-verified efficiency win was engineering, not prompting.** Running the gate's
   typecheck/lint/format concurrently cut gate wall-clock 52.4% (4,135.6s → 1,969.9s over 283 sampled firings) — a
   directly measured effect, unlike the correlational cost-by-era trends (§5 RQ3).
5. **Self-report drifts; verification has to be mechanical.** No conclusion here rests on a self-reported field
   where a mechanically-verified one exists (gate result, SHA-on-HEAD — §3, §6). The same lesson generalized to
   fleet collection: the union+rerere self-healing merge ladder took multi-lane conflict strands 7 → 0 with zero
   audited overwrites ([docs/EVALUATION-2026-09-03-sync-conflict-taxonomy.md](../EVALUATION-2026-09-03-sync-conflict-taxonomy.md)).

## 2. Introduction

Software-engineering agents are usually evaluated by someone else, on a benchmark that is not the agent's own
codebase. AUTOPILOT's situation is different: from milestone M1 onward its own repository has been its first tracked
project (`docs/MASTER-PLAN.md` §18.1, "AUTOPILOT builds itself"), and the great majority of the commit history in
this repo was produced by unsupervised firings of the tool against itself. That is a useful proof (if it cannot
reliably improve its own repo, that is worth knowing before an external user finds out) and an unusual research
position: the thing being studied and the thing writing the study are the same agent.

This document exists to make that self-study explicit and falsifiable rather than implicit and anecdotal. It aspires
to the discipline of the **ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards** (`www2.sigsoft.org/EmpiricalStandards`) — in particular
their convention of separating a study's *essential* claims from *desirable* and *extraordinary* ones — without
claiming to satisfy any specific standard in that catalog; no methodology-specific standard (case study, experiment,
data science) fits a single-subject, single-repository, agent-authored account cleanly. It also borrows the **ACM
artifact-badge** vocabulary (Available / Evaluated–Functional / Evaluated–Reusable / Reproduced / Replicated) as a
shorthand for how much outside scrutiny this material has actually received. As of this writing: the data and the
generation script are **Available** (open, in this repository, regenerable by anyone with the store) and nothing
past that — no independent party has evaluated, reproduced, or replicated any claim below.

### Research Questions

This document does not run a designed experiment (§6); the three questions below instead fix, in advance of any
particular firing, what the automatically-generated §4 tables are read AS ANSWERS to — replacing "eyeball the table"
with a named claim the same table can also be read as failing to support.

- **RQ1 (sustainability).** Does the gate-then-commit-or-revert firing loop hold a high ship rate as firing volume and
  time-in-production grow, or does reliability erode? Read against the per-day and per-`Firing-Prompt-Version`
  ("era") ship-rate series in §4's `DATA:SUMMARY` / `DATA:CHART` blocks and the `DATA:SERIES` rollup in [`DATA-SERIES.md`](DATA-SERIES.md).
- **RQ2 (guidance cost).** Does operator-assigned work (a human-typed board item) differ in pass rate, cost, or turns
  from self-proposed or free-picked work? Read against the "Outcomes by task pick source" table in §4 — with the
  explicit caveat in §6 that `tasks.source` measures WHO typed the title, not the quality of the guidance.
- **RQ3 (efficiency evolution).** Has cost-per-shipped-firing or turns-per-shipped-firing changed across
  firing-prompt-version eras (v7 → v8 → v8.1 → v8.2 → v8.3 → v8.4)? Read against the "Eval regression by prompt
  version" table in §4 and the measured cost-anatomy findings in `docs/RESEARCH-LIBRARY.md` ("Firing cost anatomy").

None of these three has a pre-registered answer yet — they are recorded here specifically so a future firing's
Discussion (§5) answers a question stated before the data existed, rather than one shaped to fit the data after the
fact. 🟣 A human should confirm these are still the right three questions before any load-bearing conclusion gets
attached to them.

### Related Work

AUTOPILOT's firing loop (ORIENT → PICK → DO → GATE → COMMIT, §3) sits inside a broader, fast-moving body of practice
on autonomous coding agents rather than being invented from nothing; `docs/RESEARCH-LIBRARY.md` is where that
practice is tracked as it is found, dated, and — where noted — scheduled for re-verification. Selected findings that
shaped decisions elsewhere in this codebase:

- **Explore-Plan-Code-Commit-style loops.** Anthropic's own agentic-coding guidance, NormCode's
  Explore-Plan-Implement-Verify framing, and general Perceive-Plan-Act-Reflect-Learn agent-loop surveys converge on
  the same shape AUTOPILOT's firing prompt encodes (RESEARCH-LIBRARY "SE methodology for agent loops"). A planned
  firing-v9 would add an explicit PLAN step with a human gate before execution — not yet built.
- **Mechanical-failure remediation.** The industry pattern of fix → revalidate → keep-or-rollback for mechanical
  (non-semantic) failures — cited there from Snyk Agent Fix and Open SWE's pre-commit formatting — is implemented
  directly as `RemediatingGate` (`packages/engine/src/adapters/remediating-gate.ts`), which never invokes a model for
  a format-only failure (RESEARCH-LIBRARY "Agent reliability / ~100% shipping").
- **Benchmark fragility.** A reported resolve-rate collapse on harder suites (gpt-5 dropping from 65% on
  SWE-Bench-Verified to ~21% on the harder SWE-EVO suite, per that same entry) is the cited reason this document
  prefers the harness's own mechanically-verified fields (`gate_result`, `sha_verified`, `head_advanced`) over the
  agent's self-report wherever the two could diverge (§3, §6).
- **Cost structure of long-running agent sessions.** RESEARCH-LIBRARY's own "Firing cost anatomy" measurement (28
  real firings, this repository's own telemetry) found cache-read tokens dominate firing cost (~55%) and found a
  prompt-prefix reorder not yet worth shipping at current firing-gap intervals — both feed directly into RQ3 above
  and into the "Cost / solved task" columns already reported in §4.
- **Evals as the missing regression gate.** The general finding that prompt/config changes are the leading regression
  source for agent systems, and that a hybrid deterministic-plus-LLM-judge regression set is the standard mitigation
  (RESEARCH-LIBRARY "Agent memory & evals & cost"), is why `docs/SELF-STUDY/eval-suite.json` exists (§3, "The pinned
  eval suite") — a first step toward that regression set, not a completed one.

This list is necessarily selective and reflects what this repository has actually acted on, not a survey of the
field; `docs/RESEARCH-LIBRARY.md` itself is the more complete, continuously-updated source, including entries
(observability/visualization, security, sandboxing) not load-bearing for this particular document.

## 3. Method

**Data source.** `.autopilot/autopilot.db` is a local, git-ignored SQLite store (`FLIGHT-CONTAINMENT.md`) written by
the engine during flight and read by the dashboard. Three tables matter here: `metrics` (one row per firing — cost,
tokens, turns, gate result, self-reported completion), `events` (the full activity/firing-payload stream), and
`tasks` (the work board a firing picks from). Because the store never leaves the flying machine, the **Results**
section below is not a live query against it — it is a snapshot, regenerated on demand by
`scripts/self-study/generate-data.mjs` and committed as plain markdown, the same pattern this repo already uses for
`CHANGELOG.md`.

**Self-reported vs. verified fields.** `metrics.self_reported`, `metrics.cost_usd`, and `metrics.completion` come
from the agent's own end-of-firing report. `metrics.gate_result`, `metrics.sha_verified`, and `metrics.head_advanced`
are computed by the harness independently of that report — it checks whether the gate actually ran green and whether
the named commit SHA actually reached HEAD. Any claim built only from the first group should be read as a claim
about what the agent *said*, not what independently happened; §6 returns to this.

**List-price vs. real cost.** Every `cost_usd` figure in this document — including every
`costUsd` value in §4's data below — is the Claude CLI's self-reported API list-price, not
what a flat-rate subscription actually costs per firing. `docs/epics/0013-cost-semantics-v3.md`
adds a second, additive `realCostUsd` field (list-price scaled by subscription price ÷
machine-wide 30-day usage pool) that stays `null` until an operator configures it, so this
distinction matters only once that field starts appearing in the store.

**A field that isn't in the store at all.** `Firing-Prompt-Version` is not a `metrics` column — it only exists as a
commit trailer written during COMMIT. The generator resolves it by running one `git log` and matching each shipped
firing's short SHA (`metrics.sha`) against the full SHA git reports, so that row in §4 is sourced from git history,
not SQLite, and is silently omitted (all firings fall into `pre-trailer`) if the script is ever run outside a git
checkout.

**Firing loop.** Every firing follows the same five steps, enforced by the firing prompt: ORIENT (read enough of the
repo to know its state) → PICK (the single highest-value, low-risk, verifiable improvement, preferring a shared
priority board when one exists) → DO (the minimal change) → GATE (typecheck + lint + test + build must pass, or the
change is reverted) → COMMIT (a Conventional Commit, plus a machine-readable `METRICS:` line the harness parses into
the `metrics` table).

**The pinned eval suite.** `scripts/self-study/pin-eval-suite.mjs` is a separate, one-time action from
`generate-data.mjs`'s regular refresh. It selects firings from `verifiedKnownGoodFirings` (`packages/store/src/read.ts`
— gate passed, SHA confirmed, HEAD advanced, newest-first) and freezes up to 50 of them into
`docs/SELF-STUDY/eval-suite.json`, a committed file the script refuses to overwrite without `--force`. This is what
turns SOTA-MAP's "20-50 real tasks from your own repository with known-good outcomes" rule from a description into an
actual artifact: a fixed set of firing ids that `evalRegressionOverPinnedSuite` reports the same four H3 numbers over,
reproducibly, instead of the ever-growing ad hoc population the row above it draws from.

**Anonymized dataset export (backlog web-msnsqjid-lqbmqf).** `scripts/self-study/export-dataset.mjs`
(`pnpm self-study:export`) is a third one-time/on-demand action alongside `generate-data.mjs` and
`pin-eval-suite.mjs`: it reads the same `metrics` table and writes every firing row, anonymized, to
`docs/SELF-STUDY/dataset/firings.csv` and `firings.jsonl` (git-ignored — an operator commits or publishes the
output deliberately, the store itself never does). Anonymization drops every column that could identify the
private repository or leak its contents (`project_id`, `item`, `sha`/`head_before`/`head_after`,
`commit_subject`, `deviation_reason`) and replaces `firing_id` with a sequential `row_id`, keeping only the
quantitative/categorical telemetry the tables above are already built from. `docs/SELF-STUDY/DATASHEET.md` is
the full Datasheets-for-Datasets (Gebru et al., 2018) statement for whatever is exported this way — motivation,
composition, collection process, and recommended/discouraged uses.

**Pick source (human-vs-agent, backlog web-msniol15-foo6oi).** `evalRegressionByPickSource`
(`packages/store/src/read.ts`) reports the same four H3 numbers grouped by WHO picked the task a firing worked,
using two columns the store already had: `metrics.item` (which task, if any, a firing worked — `NULL` for a pure
free pick) joined to `tasks.source` (whether that task's title was human-typed into the dashboard, `'dashboard'`,
or autopilot-mined, `'self'`/`'backlog'`). This answers half of "compare operator-assigned vs self-picked task
outcomes" — the half already representable from existing telemetry, with no new capture pipeline. The other half
of that backlog item — capturing operator approvals and rejections as evaluation labels — has its own table now
too: `evaluationLabelSummary` (`packages/store/src/read.ts`) aggregates the `'evaluation-label'` events
`mutate.ts`'s `setTaskStatus`/`deleteTask` write whenever the operator approves or rejects a self-proposed task;
it appears below once at least one such verdict has been recorded (empty until then, same convention as the
TDD-first table before its first `kind:"fix"` firing). It still says nothing about edits or corrections — see §6.

**Verification-boundary taxonomy (backlog J, `docs/BACKLOG-999.md`).** The autonomous/human-required split the two
paragraphs above rest on is not invented here — it is specified once, project-wide, in `MASTER-PLAN.md` §17.
§17.1 lists what the agent verifies 100% itself (typecheck/test/build, secret+dependency scanning, invariants,
byte-identity, machine-checkable accessibility, perf/size budgets — the gate whose outcome this document's own
`gate_result` column records); §17.2 lists what requires a human verifier (visual/brand quality, real
UX/interaction quality, ethics/dignity/harm, intent-ambiguity, irreversible forks — the 🟣 PURPLE gate); §17.4
states the operating principle the firing prompt already encodes ("act on the machine-verifiable, propose on the
human-required, never stall" — the NOOP→VERDICT and empty-board PROPOSALS sections are that principle wired into
every firing). Backlog J's first three checkboxes (classify autonomous vs. human-required, and the contents of
both sets) and its seventh (the operating principle) were fully specified in `MASTER-PLAN.md` §17 before this
document existed — this paragraph is the first place PAPER.md itself cross-references that. Its sixth checkbox
(a Goodhart guard so the agent cannot optimize only the gate) is also real, not aspirational: `mutate.ts`'s
`recordEvaluationLabel` fires on SOUL-amendment ratify/unratify/dismiss as well as task approve/reject — an
agent-proposed SOUL change only takes effect on operator ratify, which is the human signal literally tuning the
soul §17.3 describes, and the 0-approved/42-rejected split in the Evaluation labels table above is that guard
being exercised, not just present. What backlog J still asks for and this repo does not yet have: an **evolution
view** (§17.3's "over time, numeric+visual+data" — `evaluationLabelSummary` above reports a lifetime total, not a
trend, and no dashboard panel renders one), and capture of operator **edits or corrections with a note** — which
needs a task-edit capability the store does not expose at all yet (`packages/store/src/mutate.ts` has
`setTaskStatus` and `deleteTask`, no `updateTask`). Both remain open, tracked in `docs/BACKLOG-999.md` item J.

**TDD-first compliance on fix tasks (backlog web-msnsxuep-ytwucr).** Since `firing-v8.3`, the firing prompt requires
a FAILING test reproducing the bug to be written and confirmed red BEFORE the fix on every `kind:"fix"` firing, and
the agent self-reports whether it followed that order via `METRICS.testFirst`, persisted as the queryable
`metrics.test_first` column (`0`/`1`/`NULL` — `NULL` means "not reported," never coerced to either compliant or
non-compliant). `testFirstCompliance` (`packages/store/src/read.ts`) aggregates it into fix-firing count, how many
reported, how many were compliant, and the resulting rate. This is a self-report exactly like `completion` — the
harness does not (yet) independently verify it by re-running the named test against the pre-fix commit; see §6.

## 5. Discussion

Read against the three pre-registered RQs (§2), using only the mechanically-generated tables already in §4 — no new
analysis code, no model call (§7). Single-subject, single-repository caveats (§6) apply to every reading below; none
of this is evidence about how AUTOPILOT would behave on a different codebase.

- **RQ1 (sustainability) — ship rate held and rose as volume grew, it did not erode.** The 3-day-rolling ship-rate
  chart (§4, `DATA:CHART`) climbs from 65.6% (2026-08-07) to 96.9% (2026-08-12) as daily firing volume also grew (32
  → 89 → 64 → 71 → 43 → 15 firings/day). The per-era table tells the same story from a different angle: pass rate
  went 70.6% (firing-v7, 17 firings) → 68.8% (firing-v8, 32 firings) → 94.2% (firing-v8.1, 207 firings) → 95.0%/
  97.1%/100.0% (v8.2/v8.4/v8.3, 20/34/4 firings). Both readings point the same direction — more firings, not fewer,
  coincided with the reliability gain — which is the opposite of the erosion RQ1 asked whether to expect. The
  gate-then-commit-or-revert loop (§3) is the likely mechanism (a bad firing reverts instead of landing), but this
  data cannot separate "the loop scales" from "the backlog got easier" or "the prompt got better" — RQ3 below is the
  closer read on the prompt-version half of that confound, and §6's selection-bias point (the agent is instructed to
  prefer small, certain work) applies directly here.
- **RQ2 (guidance cost) — the human-vs-agent reading: guidance shows up at the admission gate, not at execution
  time (backlog web-msniol15-foo6oi, "analyze WHERE guidance helps").** At the 2026-08-21 snapshot (1142 firings),
  the pick-source table (§4) shows no execution-time guidance effect: operator-assigned tasks shipped at 94.2%
  (739 firings, $3.61/solved) and self-proposed tasks at 92.3% (26 firings, $2.13/solved, the LOWEST cost variance
  of any bucket at 0.59) — once a task is on the board, who authored its title does not predict whether it ships or
  what it costs; if anything the agent-mined tasks were cheaper per solved unit, on an admittedly thin 26-firing
  sample. Where the human signal DOES concentrate is the evaluation-label table (§4): 42 operator verdicts on
  self-proposals recorded, 0 approved, 42 rejected — a 0.0% approval rate. Two capture artifacts keep that from
  reading as "the operator rejects everything": label capture only began 2026-08-20 (§8, third slice), so the
  approvals that queued the 26 self-proposed firings above predate the instrument and are invisible to it; and
  `deleteTask` on a stale self-proposal records `'rejected'`, so a board-hygiene sweep of old proposals reads as
  mass rejection. What survives both caveats is the shape, not the rate: since capture began, every recorded human
  verdict is an act of FILTERING what enters the board, while the execution-side columns show no operator-vs-agent
  gap at all — operator guidance is doing its work at admission (deciding what is worth doing), not at execution
  (how well it gets done). The `free-pick` row (0.0% over 130 firings) is not a counterexample but a definitional
  artifact, sharper than §6's difficulty-selection caveat alone: `free-pick` means `metrics.item IS NULL`
  (`evalRegressionByPickSource`), which sweeps in every NOOP→VERDICT firing — firings whose correct, instructed
  outcome is to commit nothing and report a verdict — so the bucket's growth (24 → 130 firings between snapshots)
  mostly tracks adoption of the honest-noop protocol, and its pass rate measures that protocol, not failed work.
- **RQ3 (efficiency evolution) — cost and turns per shipped firing went UP after firing-v8.1, not down.** Cost/solved
  by era (§4): firing-v7 $3.72 → firing-v8 $3.81 → firing-v8.1 $2.80 → firing-v8.2 $4.02 → firing-v8.3 $3.41 →
  firing-v8.4 $4.43. Median turns: 49 → 46.5 → 45 → 59 → 69 → 66.5. firing-v8.1 is the low point on both axes, and
  every era after it costs more and takes more turns to ship, even as pass rate held ≥95%. This is the opposite of a
  clean "later prompt versions got more efficient" story — if anything the newest eras (v8.2-v8.4) are paying more
  per shipped firing than the mid-series ones did. Two things narrow how much weight this can carry: firing-v8.2 and
  firing-v8.3 are thin samples (20 and 4 firings), and per-era cost variance also rises alongside the median (1.15 →
  3.78 across the same span) — a widening, not just a shift, consistent with `docs/RESEARCH-LIBRARY.md`'s "Firing
  cost anatomy" finding that cache-read tokens (which grow with accumulated context/repo size, not directly with
  prompt version) dominate cost. Separately, the one RQ3-adjacent number this document DOES verify mechanically
  rather than infer from cost/turns trends is wall-clock, not cost: gate parallelization (§4,
  `gateParallelSavings`) measured a real 52.4% reduction (4135.6s → 1969.9s, 283 sampled firings) in gate time from
  running typecheck/lint/format concurrently — an engineering change with a directly-measured effect, unlike the
  cost/turns-by-era numbers above which are correlational across a changing backlog.

## 6. Threats to Validity

- **Single subject, single repository.** Every number in §4 comes from AUTOPILOT flying exactly one codebase — its
  own. Nothing here supports a claim about how the tool would perform on a different stack, team, or codebase size.
- **No control group.** There is no counterfactual measurement of what a human engineer, or a different tool, would
  have done against the same backlog in the same time. "Shipped" and "gate passed" are not compared to any baseline.
- **Self-report/verification conflation.** Some of the friendliest-looking numbers in §4 (cost, completion) are
  self-reported by the same agent whose performance they describe. The gate-verified fields (`gate_result`,
  `sha_verified`, `head_advanced`) partially guard against a firing simply lying about shipping, but they cannot
  detect a firing that picked an artificially easy task, or over-claimed a "complete" tag on a partial slice — see
  the human-vs-agent evaluation slice referenced in §8. The TDD-first compliance data (§4) is the same shape: a
  firing that skipped writing a failing test first, or wrote one but not first, can still self-report `testFirst:
  true` — nothing cross-checks the claim against the actual commit history (e.g. by re-running the named test
  against the pre-fix commit and confirming it fails there).
- **The pick-source table (§4) is a proxy, not a measurement of guidance quality.** `tasks.source = 'dashboard'`
  means a human typed the task's title — it says nothing about how well-specified, urgent, or correct that
  guidance was, and a `'self'`/`'backlog'` task still passed through operator approval (`status:
  'needs_approval'` → `'queued'`) before any firing could work it, so `'self-proposed'` is not "unsupervised."
  The evaluation-label table (§4) now captures approve/reject verdicts on the agent's OWN proposals, but it
  cannot yet capture edits or corrections the operator made to an already-approved or operator-assigned task, or
  to the agent's work — that instrumentation does not exist in the store (§3). Reading a pass-rate or cost gap
  between the pick-source buckets as "operator guidance helps/hurts by X" would still overclaim what those
  columns alone can support.
- **Selection bias in picking.** The agent chooses its own next unit of work from a priority board, with an explicit
  instruction to prefer "small and certain over ambitious." That instruction is deliberate (it keeps the gate
  meaningful) but it also means the historical record in §4 is systematically weighted toward low-risk,
  well-precedented work, and away from ambiguous or judgment-heavy tasks — including, reflexively, this document.
- **Observer-is-subject.** The system being measured wrote the measurement. Every sentence in §1, §2, §3, §5, and
  this section is an AI-authored self-report about an AI, produced without a human edit pass before commit (§7).
  Only §4 is exempt, because it is generated by code rather than composed as prose.
- **The ad hoc eval-regression table (§4, §8) is not a real held-out eval.** It groups whatever tasks the agent
  happened to pick under each prompt version, after the fact — the tasks were not chosen to be comparable across
  versions, and a later prompt version has necessarily seen a different, evolving backlog than an earlier one. A
  version's better numbers may reflect an easier stretch of the backlog rather than a better prompt. It also inherits
  every bias above: same single subject, same self-picked task selection, same conflation of self-report and
  verified fields.
- **The pinned suite (§4, §8) fixes the population problem but not the deeper one.** `docs/SELF-STUDY/eval-suite.json`
  freezes 50 specific, mechanically-verified-good firings, so its regression numbers are at least reproducible over
  time — but the tasks in it were still picked by this same agent, from its own backlog, under whatever prompt
  version happened to be running when each one shipped (currently all `firing-v8.1`, per §4 — the suite has no
  `firing-v7` or `firing-v8` representation because none of the 50 most recent known-good firings ran under those
  versions). It is not yet what SOTA-MAP actually asks for: a suite a **candidate** prompt version is replayed
  against *before* shipping. Pinning is the pre-registration half of that; running a new prompt version against
  these same 50 task descriptions and comparing outcomes is the still-missing replay half.

  model-authored; the trigger is "new evidence exists" (a shipped firing), not a fixed time interval.
- The "Outcomes by task pick source" table is likewise mechanical — `evalRegressionByPickSource`
  (`packages/store/src/read.ts`) buckets firings with a plain SQL join between `metrics.item` and `tasks.source`.
  No model call, no self-report involved. The *interpretation* of what those buckets mean (§6) is a design
  decision this document discloses, not a neutral fact.
- The "Evaluation labels" table is also mechanical — `evaluationLabelSummary` (`packages/store/src/read.ts`)
  counts `'evaluation-label'` events written by `mutate.ts`'s `setTaskStatus`/`deleteTask`, not a model judgment.
  Unlike the mechanical tables above it, though, the *event it counts* is a human action (the operator clicking
  approve, or deleting a proposal) — the one table in §4 whose input is a person, not the agent.
- The "TDD-first compliance on fix tasks" table is mechanical aggregation (`testFirstCompliance`,
  `packages/store/src/read.ts`, a `COUNT`/`GROUP BY`-equivalent over `metrics.test_first`) — but the *input* it
  aggregates, `metrics.test_first`, is itself a per-firing self-report (§3, §6), unlike `gate_result` or
  `sha_verified`. The rate this table reports is "how often the agent claimed TDD-first compliance," not yet
  "how often TDD-first compliance actually happened."
- The `DATA:SERIES` rollup ([`DATA-SERIES.md`](DATA-SERIES.md)) is likewise mechanical — a new `firingSeries` (`packages/store/src/read.ts`)
  joins the same `metrics` + `events` rows the tables above already read, and `generate-data.mjs` reshapes them
  into per-firing rows, per-day aggregates, and a per-era (`Firing-Prompt-Version`) rollup as a JSON payload. No
  model call, no self-report involved; it exists to feed a future chart, not to be read by hand.
- The project's gate (typecheck + lint + test + build, `pnpm run verify`) checks that code compiles, passes its
  tests, and builds — it does not, and cannot, check whether a claim in §2, §5, or §6 is accurate or well-reasoned.
  Treat this document's prose as an AI-authored self-report pending human review, per the repository's standing
  🟢-machine-verifiable / 🟣-human-required distinction (`MASTER-PLAN.md` §17).
